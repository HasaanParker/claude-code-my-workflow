\chapter{Aid Competition across Spheres of Influence: Quantitative Evidence}
\label{ch:quant}

The empirical core of the dissertation. Short-run allocation models, an
instrumental-variables strategy for Chinese aid exposure, and a long-run
dynamics test are brought to bear on whether US and Chinese aid race or diverge
across spheres of influence. The short answer, developed over the sections
below, is that the sign flip H1 predicts does not appear under any
operationalization tried here, that what positive association remains is
sensitive to how differential trends are handled, and that the instrument
intended to settle the question has no first stage.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:quant-data}
The panel merges two sources at the recipient-country (ISO-3) $\times$ year
level: OECD-DAC Creditor Reporting System (DAC3A) commitments for US official
development assistance (ODA), 1970--2022, constant 2022 USD; and AidData Global
Chinese Development Finance (GCDF) 3.0, project-level Chinese official finance
aggregated to country-year, commitment years 2000--2021. Two Chinese-finance
measures are carried in parallel: \texttt{china\_all} (all
recommended-for-aggregates flows -- ODA-like, OOF-like, and vague official
finance) and \texttt{china\_oda} (ODA-like flows only). The balanced panel spans
150 US-recipient countries, 1970--2022 (7{,}950 country-years); Chinese finance
is 0 by construction outside 2000--2021 and for country-years with no recorded
project. Region is assigned from ISO-3 via continent, not the GCDF
\texttt{Recipient Region} field, so it is defined identically for both donors.
Controls (GDP per capita, population; World Bank WDI) and a UNGA ideal-point
alignment distance from the US (Voeten, Sep.\ 2023 release; session $\to$ year
via session $1=1946$) are merged in as the sphere-of-influence ingredient
introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:theory} -- an alignment covariate, not yet the
finished SOI construct.

\section{Empirical strategy}
\label{sec:quant-strategy}
The baseline is a short-run allocation model with country and year fixed
effects:
\[
\log(\text{US aid}_{i,t} + 1) = \beta \log(\text{China aid}_{i,t-1} + 1) + \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \varepsilon_{i,t}
\]
fit pooled and separately by region, for both China measures, with
country-clustered standard errors. $\beta$ is a short-run elasticity of US aid
to lagged Chinese finance -- the sign test for H1 (race vs.\ withdrawal). An
extended specification adds $\log(\text{gdppc})$, $\log(\text{pop})$, and the
lagged US-alignment distance as controls. IV (Dreher et al.\ shift-share:
Chinese steel production $\times$ recipient exposure) and the panel-VAR /
Granger test of long-run co-evolution are developed in
\texttt{analyses/03\_iv\_robustness/} and \texttt{analyses/04\_var\_dynamics/}
respectively.

\paragraph{Estimation sample.} All estimates below are fit on \textbf{2000--2021},
not the full 1970--2022 panel. GCDF~3.0 covers commitment years 2000--2021 only,
so Chinese finance is zero for 1970--1999 \emph{by construction} -- that is an
absence of coverage, not an absence of finance. Those rows carry no treatment
variation but still shape the fixed effects, so a recipient whose US aid trended
upward across the same decades in which Chinese finance first appeared registers
as a positive response. Estimating on the full panel inflates every elasticity
in this chapter, in some cases by a factor of three.\footnote{Pre-China-baseline
US-aligned bloc: $0.251$ on the full panel, $0.082$ on 2000--2021. Europe:
$0.493$ vs.\ $0.229$. See the 2026-09-15 session log.} The restriction also
brings this chapter into line with the panel VAR of \S\ref{sec:quant-var},
which was specified on 2000--2021 from the outset.

\paragraph{Reading against H1.} With standard errors clustered by country and
the sample restricted as above, the all-official-finance elasticity is positive
almost everywhere but reaches conventional significance only in the pooled
sample ($0.053$, $p=.007$).            %% source: analyses/01_quant_main/scripts/R/03_analyze.R
Europe is marginal ($0.229$, $p=.064$); Africa ($0.039$, $p=.317$), Americas
($0.046$, $p=.175$), Asia ($0.015$, $p=.519$), and Oceania ($0.061$, $p=.383$)
are not distinguishable from zero. \emph{Africa is not a significant positive
effect on this vintage} -- a genuine discrepancy with the Dec 2024 prospectus's
reported $+0.113$ Africa-only pattern (negative elsewhere): the point estimate's
direction still matches, but the magnitude does not, Africa is not
distinguishable from zero, and no region shows the negative coefficient the
``withdrawal outside Africa'' half of H1 requires. Table~\ref{tab:controls}
(controls lagged like the treatment, avoiding a predetermined-vs-contemporaneous
mismatch) leaves the pooled effect intact but smaller ($0.039$, $p=.015$) and
removes Europe's marginal significance. Candidate explanations for the vintage
discrepancy -- not adjudicated -- are the GCDF release (the prospectus likely
used 2.0 rather than 3.0) and the US ODA input file (the working recipe notes a
since-superseded \texttt{usa oda commits.csv} was swapped for the current DAC3A
extract). None of this was tuned to force a match to $+0.113$, per the project's
replication and anti-p-hacking discipline. \textbf{Caveat on Europe and
Oceania:} both cluster on only 11 countries, below the conventional 20--50
minimum for cluster-robust asymptotics, so read those cells cautiously
regardless of point estimate.

\subsection{Re-operationalizing sphere of influence}
\label{sec:quant-soi}
Section~\ref{sec:soi-operational} replaces continent with an alignment-based
SOI bloc (China-aligned / Contested / US-aligned, from UNGA ideal-point
distance, terciled \emph{within the US-aid-recipient sample} and within year,
lagged -- not against the full $\sim$190-country UN membership, so blocs mean
``relative to other aid recipients,'' not ``relative to Canada and the
UK'').\footnote{Checked as a robustness question, not a free design choice:
terciling against the full UN membership instead reproduces the same
qualitative pattern (if anything with a slightly larger US-aligned
coefficient and looser $p$-value), so this is a parsimony choice -- it needs
no extra justification for the reference population -- not one that changes
the finding. See the 2026-09-14 session log.} Table~\ref{tab:soi} re-runs the
identical baseline specification on this cut instead of region.

\paragraph{This is not the pattern H1 predicts.} H1 predicts the race
(significant positive elasticity) in \emph{Contested} spheres and withdrawal
(negative) where China has effectively already won the recipient
(\emph{China-aligned}). Instead: Contested is not significant
(all-official-finance: $0.030$, $p=.193$)                %% source: analyses/01_quant_main/scripts/R/03_analyze.R
; China-aligned is null ($0.011$, $p=.546$), not the predicted negative; and
the largest and only significant elasticity is in \textbf{US-aligned}
countries ($0.071$, $p=.020$) -- recipients where the US has \emph{already}
secured alignment, not the contested middle.

\paragraph{The endogeneity test, and what it revealed.} UN-voting alignment is
itself partly \emph{caused} by US aid, so a one-year lag does not sever the
loop: a country is US-aligned at $t-1$ partly because of US aid at $t-2$,
$t-3$, and that relationship persists into $t$. To break this, bloc assignment
was re-frozen on a pre-China baseline (mean \texttt{rel\_align} over
1990--1999, terciled once, time-invariant, so it is predetermined relative to
every China-era flow tested; 135 of 150 recipients have sufficient pre-period
coverage). The US-aligned result survives that test --- $0.082$, $p=.018$,
with Contested ($0.005$) and China-aligned ($0.035$) both null --- and
leave-one-country-out across all 45 US-aligned recipients is stable, so it is
not an outlier artifact.

\textbf{It does not, however, survive country-specific linear trends}
(Table~\ref{tab:soi-trends}): the pooled elasticity falls from $0.053$ to
$-0.002$ ($p=.900$), and US-aligned from $0.082$ to $0.025$ ($p=.158$). This
is the honest boundary of the result. Country trends can over-absorb --- if
Chinese finance grows smoothly within a recipient and US aid responds
progressively, the trend consumes the effect --- so this is reported as
robustness rather than as the primary specification. But the range it implies
is wide: somewhere between a modest positive association and nothing. The
panel VAR of \S\ref{sec:quant-var}, which finds no lagged cross-donor effect
in either direction on the same window, is consistent with the lower end of
that range.

\paragraph{Reading this against H1.} Two things can be said with confidence and
one cannot. \emph{With confidence:} under two different, non-overlapping
operationalizations of ``sphere of influence'' -- continent
(\S\ref{sec:quant-results}) and UN-voting alignment, the latter in both a
$t-1$ and a fully predetermined pre-China form -- the short-run lagged-FE
strategy does not recover the sign flip H1 predicts. No cut produces a
significant race in the contested middle, and no cut produces the significant
negative that ``withdrawal'' requires. \emph{Also with confidence:} whatever
positive association exists is concentrated among recipients already aligned
with the US, not among contested ones.

\emph{What cannot yet be said} is whether that concentration is a finding or an
artifact of differential trending. A candidate reading is
\emph{reinforcement} -- the US topping up recipients it has already secured
when China enters, rather than contesting the middle -- which would be a
different mechanism from H1, not a weaker version of it. The composition of the
US-aligned bloc is suggestive on this point: it is dominated by
NATO/EU-accession-track and post-Soviet states, Pacific compact states, and the
Western Hemisphere, i.e.\ places where the US has standing institutional
architecture rather than places where alignment is genuinely up for grabs. But
the trends specification removes the effect, and a mechanism story should not
be built on an estimate that a standard robustness check can erase.
Adjudicating this requires identification the current design does not supply --
the instrumental-variables strategy of \S\ref{sec:quant-iv} is the natural next
step. See the 2026-09-14 and 2026-09-15 session logs.

\section{Instrumental-variables robustness}
\label{sec:quant-iv}
The estimates above are associational: they cannot distinguish a US response to
Chinese entry from Chinese entry into places US aid was already moving. The
standard fix in this literature is the shift-share instrument used by Dreher,
Fuchs, Parks, Strange and Tierney (2021): interact a national, year-varying
measure of China's supply-side
capacity with a recipient-specific measure of exposure to Chinese finance,
\[
Z_{i,t} = \ln(\text{Chinese crude steel production}_{t-1}) \times \text{exposure}_i .
\]
Steel production comes from the World Steel Association series in their
replication package (1990--2015, which bounds this section's sample to
2000--2015); exposure is built from Bartke's historical record of Chinese aid
as the share of 1970--1999 years in which a recipient received a Chinese
project. That pre-period share is \emph{predetermined} relative to every flow
tested here, which is the property a shift-share design requires.

\input{tables/table_iv.tex}     %% source: analyses/03_iv_robustness/scripts/R/04_tables.R

\paragraph{The instrument does not transfer to this design.} With the
predetermined share, there is no first stage: the first-stage coefficient on
$Z_{i,t}$ is $-0.527$ ($t=-0.36$, $F=0.27$) for all official finance, and only
$F=6.3$ --- below the conventional threshold --- for ODA-like flows.       %% source: analyses/03_iv_robustness/scripts/R/03_analyze.R
The 2SLS estimates that follow are correspondingly uninformative ($-2.47$ with
a standard error of $7.65$). This is not a marginal-power problem to be
reported with caveats; it is an absence of identifying variation.

\paragraph{Why it fails, and why that is substantively interesting.}
Figure~\ref{fig:iv-first-stage} shows that pre-2000 exposure \emph{does} predict
post-2000 Chinese finance across recipients ($r=0.48$, $p<10^{-9}$). But that
predictive content is entirely cross-sectional, and the country fixed effects
this design requires absorb all of it. What remains for identification is the
interaction's within-country variation --- which, because Chinese steel
production rose near-monotonically over the window, is approximately a
country-specific linear trend scaled by exposure. Identification therefore
demands that historically-exposed recipients saw \emph{differentially steeper}
growth in Chinese finance. They did not: 68\% of recipients with no recorded
pre-2000 Chinese aid received Chinese finance anyway after 2000. China's
expansion in this period was broad-based rather than a deepening of existing
ties, and that fact --- interesting in its own right --- is precisely what
destroys the instrument.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig_iv_first_stage.pdf}
\caption{Pre-2000 Chinese-aid exposure against post-2000 Chinese finance, by recipient. The relationship is real but cross-sectional, and country fixed effects absorb it.}
\label{fig:iv-first-stage}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{A caution about the published specification.} Dreher et al.'s
headline share is computed over the \emph{study window} (2000--2014) rather
than a pre-period. Substituting that contemporaneous share here does produce a
strong first stage ($F=27.3$), but the share is then constructed from the same
post-2000 Chinese-finance variation the instrument exists to purge, so its
power is mechanical rather than evidence of identification. Notably, even
taking it at face value, the resulting 2SLS estimate is insignificant
($0.321$, $p=.196$). The contrast between the two share definitions is
reported in Table~\ref{tab:iv} for exactly this reason.

\paragraph{What this means for the chapter.} Two independent limitations now
point the same way. The instrument is approximately a country-specific trend,
so it cannot be combined with the trend specification of
\S\ref{sec:quant-soi} --- it is vulnerable to the same confound rather than a
remedy for it. And it has no first stage under the only share definition that
would make it valid. The identification problem this section was meant to
solve therefore remains open, and the associational estimates of
\S\S\ref{sec:quant-results}--\ref{sec:quant-soi} should be read as descriptive.

\section{Long-run dynamics}
\label{sec:quant-var}
The short-run model treats each year's China-aid shock as if it fully resolves
within one period. A bivariate panel VAR in $(\log \text{US aid}, \log
\text{China aid})$, fit by system GMM (Blundell-Bond; Abrigo and Love 2016)
over the double-donor era (2000--2021, 150 countries, balanced), tests whether
the relationship is a genuine lead--lag dynamic rather than a same-year
coincidence -- using the \texttt{china\_all} measure only in this pass
(\texttt{china\_oda} is a documented, not-yet-run extension).

\paragraph{Reading the VAR.} Neither lagged cross-donor term is significant:
lagged China aid does not predict future US aid ($-0.013$, $p=.40$) and lagged
US aid does not predict future China aid ($-0.075$, $p=.56$)                %% source: analyses/04_var_dynamics/scripts/R/03_analyze.R
-- each series is driven mainly by its own persistence (US: $0.475$, China:
$0.225$, both $p<.001$). The system is stable (companion-matrix eigenvalues
$0.48$ and $0.22$, inside the unit circle). This is a genuinely different
picture from Section~\ref{sec:quant-results}'s significant positive short-run
elasticities: once identification is handled via system GMM (rather than
two-way FE) and the sample is restricted to the double-donor era, there is no
detectable lagged Granger-style response in either direction. Two readings are
live and not yet adjudicated: (i) the FE elasticities capture a genuine
same-year co-movement that a one-year-lag VAR is too coarse to detect, or (ii)
the FE elasticities partly reflect a common trend/shock that year and country
FEs do not fully absorb. \textbf{Caveat:} the Hansen overidentification test
rejects ($\chi^2(28)=100.22$, $p<.001$) -- the instrument set may not be fully
valid, so the VAR point estimates should be read as suggestive pending an
instrument-count/validity check, not as a confirmed null.
